    What it does

    What changes when you use Records

    Records is the shared place your project data lives. Your tools read it and write to it. Nobody has to work out which copy is right.

    Before

    • The same project lives in Jira, a spreadsheet and a board.
    • Nobody is sure which copy is right.
    • "Who changed this?" has no answer.
    • When you stop using a tool, its data goes with it.

    With Records

    • There is one copy, and every tool works from it.
    • Each task has a history of who changed what.
    • The data belongs to your organisation, not to any one tool.
    • You can take all of it out whenever you want.

    Your tools connect

    Apps you build, Jira scripts and clients, and cloudflare-os gadgets all connect to a datastore: one shared set of projects and tasks, usually one per department.

    Records checks the change

    It checks who is asking and what they are allowed to do. The rules are the same whichever tool sent the change.

    Everyone sees it

    The change is saved and added to the history. Every connected tool shows it within seconds.

    Records is a good fit if

    • several tools or teams need the same project data
    • you need to show who changed what, and when
    • you want to own your data instead of renting it inside one app

    It is not the right choice if

    • you want a ready-made tracker app to log in to (use Jira or Linear)
    • you rely on Jira boards, sprints or attachments
    • one tool on its own already does the job

    Words we use

    Six words that explain the rest

    Datastore
    One shared set of projects and tasks. It belongs to your organisation, not to any one tool. Most teams start with one per department.
    Projects and tasks
    A project groups tasks under a short code, such as ENG. Tasks are numbered ENG-1, ENG-2 and so on. Records calls tasks "issues", as Jira does.
    History
    The list of every change to a task: who made it, when, from which tool, and the old and new values. Changes are added to it, never edited.
    Members and roles
    People you add to a datastore. Each has one role:
    • Reader can see projects and tasks.
    • Editor can also create, change and comment.
    • Admin can also add people and connect tools.
    • Owner can also archive the datastore or hand it over.
    A data administrator creates datastores for the organisation and picks their owners.
    Connection
    A tool's permission to use a datastore, limited to what it needs. An admin can remove it at any time. Removing it never removes data.
    cloudflare-os and gadgets
    cloudflare-os is a workspace where people use and build small apps, called gadgets. Records is built in to it (see below).
    What you get

    Built for data you'll be asked about later

    History on every task

    See every change to a task, who made it, and what it was before. Nobody can quietly rewrite it.

    No silent overwrites

    If two people change the same task at once, the second person is told. They see the latest version and can make their change again.

    Removing a tool never removes your data

    Stop using a gadget or an app and the projects and tasks stay. They belong to the organisation.

    Works with common Jira scripts and clients

    Point an existing Jira script at a datastore and it reads and writes tasks. Boards and sprints aren't included.

    Restore one datastore to an earlier time

    If a bad import or a broken script damages one datastore, roll it back. Every other datastore stays as it is.

    Get all your data out

    Export a whole datastore, or have your IT team read it through the API, a Jira client or your reporting tools.

    For cloudflare-os teams

    Records in cloudflare-os

    Records is built in to cloudflare-os. Here is what that means for you and your gadgets.

    What changes for my team?

    Two gadgets work from a shared datastore: Project Board and Project Report. Several boards and reports can show the same projects, and everyone sees the same tasks.

    What happens to our existing boards?

    Nothing. The ordinary Board gadget keeps its own data, and nothing is moved. Only gadgets you connect to a datastore use Records.

    Do I need to do anything?

    No. Nothing changes until someone creates a datastore and connects a gadget to it.

    Who decides who can see and edit?

    The datastore's owners and admins. They add members and set roles on the Data page in the Workshop. Your role follows you into every gadget.

    What are approvals?

    When you edit a task in a Project Board, Records saves it as your change. The person who owns the gadget in the Workshop decides which changes need approval.

    They can let everyday edits, like moving a card, save straight away. Anything else waits as "Pending approval" until they say yes.

    Can I see a task's history in the gadget?

    Not yet. The history is kept from the first change, and a later version of the Project Board will show it.

    Does it cost anything?

    No. Records is included in your cloudflare-os deployment.

    What if we remove a gadget?

    The projects and tasks stay in the datastore. Connect another gadget and carry on.

    SavingYour change shows at once and is on its way.
    Pending approvalWaiting for the gadget's owner to say yes.
    SavedEveryone can see it now.
    ConflictSomeone changed it first. You'll see their version; make your edit again if you still need it.

    Next step: ask your data administrator to create a datastore for your team, then connect a Project Board to it.

    For developers

    A backend for project data you don't have to build

    Records is a REST API over Postgres for projects, tasks and comments. It speaks a subset of Jira's API and gives you a readable change history. It runs on Cloudflare Workers or on Node, beside any Postgres 17.

    What's in the data model

    • Projects with a key (ENG) and numbered issues (ENG-12)
    • Issues: title, description, status, priority, assignee, up to 50 custom fields
    • Workflow states and the allowed moves between them
    • Comments
    • A history entry for every change, readable as a feed

    Not yet: issue types, sub-tasks, issue links, attachments, sprints.

    Which credential to use

    Service keyBackend jobs and scripts. One key per datastore, with the permissions you choose.
    OIDC tokenYour own web apps, acting as the signed-in person.
    Delegated tokenA platform such as cloudflare-os acting for a person, for 60 seconds.

    Signed webhooksAn outbox, a worker and a signing schemeIncluded, in Records or Jira format

    Limits

    • Request bodies up to 64 KiB; responses up to 1 MiB
    • 300 requests a minute per key on Records Cloud
    • Browser sync of up to 5,000 issues per datastore
    • Export of up to 10,000 issues at a time

    Security in brief

    • Service keys are shown once, stored hashed, and expire within a year.
    • Every read is checked twice: by the service and by Postgres.
    • The history can only be added to. Redacting personal data is audited.

    Questions

    What people ask first

    Is there a screen my team logs in to?

    Not for everyday work. Your team keeps using the tools they have: gadgets, your own apps, or Jira clients. Admins get a console for members, keys and webhooks.

    Do we have to leave Jira?

    No. Records can sit beside Jira. Scripts and clients that use Jira's standard task API can point at Records instead. Jira boards, sprints and attachments aren't included.

    How do we get our existing data in?

    There's no import tool yet. Your IT team can load data through the API or with a Jira script, and we'll help.

    Where is our data stored?

    Records Cloud keeps data in London. If you host Records yourself, it stays in your own database.

    What happens if two people change the same task?

    Both see their change at once. The first one to arrive is saved. The second person is told, sees the latest version, and can make their change again.

    Does it work offline?

    Only briefly. Unsent changes stay on the page, and the page warns you before you close it. Records doesn't store your business data in the browser.

    Can we get our data out?

    Yes. Export a whole datastore, or read it through the API, a Jira client, webhooks or your reporting tools.

    Do we need cloudflare-os?

    No. Records works on its own. cloudflare-os is one of the tools it works with, and the first one it was built for.
