Who Records is for

Your software should fit your organisation.

Move away from an enterprise suite

Choose a workflow, model its concepts and import or map its data. A team could move work tracking from Jira or Linear, messaging from Slack or Teams, and knowledge from Notion or Confluence. Preserve relationships and access rules, then build focused interfaces on cloudflare-os or your own frontend.

Start with one bounded workflow and rehearse migration before switching. Compatibility adapters can smooth the transition, but the open model remains useful without them. Vendor-specific behaviour still requires explicit mapping and tests.

Start a bespoke business system

A new company should not have to organise itself around the fields and screens of a software suite. Begin with shared concepts such as people, organisations and projects; add your domain’s terms and commands. Several apps can share those definitions from the beginning.

Vibe code the app, reuse the foundation

AI-assisted builders can work against a documented model, API contract and permission boundary. Generate a customer portal, an operations board or a specialist tool while reusing the same data and rules. Review generated code and grants like any other implementation.

How this relates to Supabase

Supabase provides a broad backend platform, including Postgres, authentication, storage, realtime and APIs. It is a strong starting point when you want control over those building blocks. Records chooses a more opinionated starting layer: versioned domain models, storage mappings, domain commands and app requirements.

This is a difference in abstraction and product scope, not evidence that Supabase is unsuitable for enterprise use or scales less well. Records must earn its enterprise and scaling claims through isolation, auditability, restore drills, operational ownership and repeatable load tests.

Enterprise requirements, explicit evidence

  • Scoped identities and datastore isolation, tested with real runtime roles.
  • Commands with revisions, safe retries and transactional history.
  • Versioned models and repeatable migrations.
  • Recovery procedures, resource limits and measured concurrency.
  • Provider choice and documented portability.
