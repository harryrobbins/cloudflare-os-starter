**1. The chosen name.**

Countermand Labs maps one precise word onto the "human overrides risky AI action" half of the thesis. Clean at Companies House, all four domains free, no real namesake. Won 12/15 runs, usually by a clear margin.

But it covers only the override half — nothing for "capability granted" or "flows only where it may" — yet fit scored near-perfect almost unanimously: grading on vibes, not the criterion. It reads martially: judges flagged "rare-word risk" and command-tone while voting for it. "Labs" undersells a consultancy-led business, signalling R&D not delivery. Only Companies House and domains were checked, never trademarks.

I'd pick it, provisionally: memorable, sober, ownable by the evidence gathered, the decisive winner. But I'd run a trademark search and test the martial tone with real CISOs — the panel is confident on data it never checked.

**3. How effective the process was.**

What worked: the shared dossier caught real landmines early (Hadrian, Cordon, Chancery, Barbican, Corbel, Auctra, Aegis, Sentinel — all blocked or saturated), and the planted control died round 1 every time, proving the panel discriminates. Fifteen repeats surfaced a real signal a single run couldn't.

The measures are less flattering. Effective-judges collapses to ~1.1-1.3 of ten (round-1 correlation 0.88-0.90); restricted to Companies-House-clear names, correlation still sits at 0.74-0.77 — ten models act as barely more than one judge, agreeing on shared facts, not taste. Jev, meant as an independent check, correlates 0.97 with the panel — reinforcing consensus, not diversifying it. Self-preference is real but uneven: claude-sonnet-5 is self-critical (-0.36) and the most accurate judge (0.81); gpt-5.6-luna (+0.66) and claude-haiku-4.5 (+0.56) inflate their own entries and judge worse (0.77, 0.71). Herding toward the shared mean is near zero to slightly negative, but round-to-round movement (~0.46-0.49) exceeds the final margin between the top two (0.13-0.26 in three Claustra wins).

Threats to validity: the shared dossier plus a mechanical ownable score (25%, pass/fail on CH and domains) narrow what's judged; halving 31 candidates to 16 in one noisy round risks cutting a good name on bad luck; similar-tier models compound correlation beyond the dossier; and backing reward goes flat at 1 point for rounds 5-7, so late leader-following pays like early conviction, as the data shows.

**4. One change you'd make first.**

Replace Jev with a different evaluator — a human panel, or a model briefed adversarially, not handed the judges' own brief. At 0.97 correlation, Jev buys a second vote for the same opinion, not independence. That gap must close before adversarial panels can be trusted as a method.
