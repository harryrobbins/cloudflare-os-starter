## Tally family (2026-09-24)

A follow-on from Left Tally. A tally stick recorded a debt as notches, then was split lengthways so
the halves could only be matched with each other. The English Exchequer kept its accounts this way
from the 12th century until 1826. The creditor held the longer half, the **stock** (the origin of
"stockholder"); the debtor held the shorter half, the **foil** (hence "counterfoil"). In 1834 the
obsolete sticks were burned in a stove beneath the House of Lords, and the fire destroyed the Palace
of Westminster. Medieval **chirographs** applied the same idea to parchment: two copies of a deed on
one sheet, cut apart through a word.

Gutenberg #216, with the Chinese from #7337) and the Liezi (Chinese original, Gutenberg #7341).

and no existing company with the name turned up in a web search, unless the entry says otherwise.

**Found for the pitch deck.** Giles's 1889 Zhuangzi, ch. XIX: *"Do not develop your artificial
intelligence, but develop that intelligence which is from God. From the latter, results virtue; from
the former, cunning."* (Checked against the text.) It works as an epigraph for a company whose thesis
is distrust of clever machines.

## Round 1 (2026-09-24)

Brief: sober enough for mid-market and enterprise buyers, memorable rather than generic, a little
wry but not silly; a nod to Iain M. Banks ship names is welcome if light. A `Labs` suffix is fine in
the domain and the company name. No major AI competitor with the same name. Reference points:
Outlandish, Surprisingly Limited, Fishtown Analytics, Typesafe.

The Banks idea: Culture ship names are understated phrases with dry intent ("So Much For Subtlety",
"Just Read The Instructions"). Here the phrase also tells the product story, which is an
architecture of distrust: capabilities, approvals and information that only flows where it may.

Domain results are from `namecheck.py` on 2026-09-24. Competitor notes are from a quick web search,
not a trade-mark search. Companies House checks are pending the API key.
