# Excelcion website — content plan
 
*Version 1, 26 August 2026. Covers positioning, audiences, journeys, the full sitemap with page-by-page content specs, CTA strategy, and the SEO/GEO plan. Assumes the product positioning from the project research: governed vibe-coding platform, assurance-led licence, partner-delivered implementations.*
 
## 0. What the research says works (and what this plan does about it)
 
Five findings shape everything below.
 
First, the homepage is a conversion funnel, not an information hub: it has about five seconds to answer *what problem, what product, who for*, with one primary CTA above the fold, a real product image rather than an illustration, and three to five customer logos immediately below the hero. Outcome-led headlines beat feature-led ones, and quantified, attributed testimonials beat generic praise — HubSpot's analysis of 330,000 CTAs found personalised CTAs convert 202% better than defaults. This plan gives every page one job and one primary CTA.
 
Second, hiding pricing is now a net negative for this buyer: enterprise buyers expect at least the shape of the price, and a page that shows seat prices plus "packages from £X" filters out bad-fit leads cheaply. The plan publishes seat pricing and package ranges, with "talk to us" reserved for the managed/on-prem tier.
 
Third, the way B2B buyers find vendors has changed under everyone's feet: 51% of B2B software buyers now *start* vendor research in an AI assistant (up from 29% a year earlier), 73% use AI somewhere in evaluation, and AI-referred visitors convert at 4.4x organic. Getting cited by the engines is a content-shape problem: 44.2% of citations come from the first 30% of a page, extraction-friendly formatting triples citation likelihood, and 83% of commercial citations come from pages updated in the last 12 months. So every page below leads with a direct, quotable answer; comparison and FAQ content is written to be lifted; and the plan includes a quarterly refresh cadence.
 
Fourth, for a security-led product the classic blockers are the CISO and procurement, and the sites that win give them a self-service trust centre (certs, sub-processors, DPA, questionnaire answers) so the champion never has to ask us for a PDF.
 
Fifth, a ten-person company cannot write forever: the SEO plan is built on two programmatic templates (industries and applications) plus a small number of essays that do the reputation work, rather than a general blog.
 
## 1. Positioning and message architecture
 
**Category line (for the H1s and the meta descriptions):** the governed AI workspace — or, spelled out, "the platform that lets your people build AI tools on your business data, safely."
 
**Message hierarchy**, used in this order everywhere:
 
1. **Problem:** your people are already using AI on company data, ungoverned (83% of UK organisations report shadow AI). Blanket Copilot seats are expensive and barely used; blocking AI just pushes it underground.
2. **Product:** Excelcion gives every team a workspace where they can build AI tools, apps and automations on your actual business data — and every agent, and everything built with one, inherits your access rules. Capabilities not keys; per-user sandboxes; human sign-off on real actions; a full audit trail.
3. **Proof:** the reference customer(s), the open-source foundations, the certifications, the founders' delivery record.
4. **Price honesty:** seat prices on the page; "half the licence cost of a blanket Copilot rollout for a typical mix, and you control the model spend" — never a naked "half price, 5x productivity" claim, which the government trial data would demolish in any serious buyer's hands.
**Tone:** plain English, engineer-written, no AI-hype vocabulary ("revolutionise", "unleash", "supercharge" are banned words). Confidence comes from specifics. The Jamie-Lord-essay register — architecture of distrust — is the brand voice for the technical material.
 
## 2. Target users
 
**P1 — The accountable technology leader** (CTO/CDO/IT Director, 150–3,000-person firm, tier-1 sectors). Owns the AI decision and the risk. Pain: board pressure to "do AI", shadow AI, Copilot bill scepticism. Needs: a governance story they can defend, a price they can justify, a vendor who will implement. Converts on: scoping call.
**P2 — The CISO / security lead.** Rarely the entry point, always the veto. Reads the security architecture page and the trust centre only. Needs: certifications, sub-processor list, data-flow answers, deployment options. Converts on: nothing — their job is to stop saying no. Success is a downloaded security pack.
**P3 — The operations owner** (COO/practice director at an agency, AEC firm, consultancy). Doesn't care about MCP; cares that client A's data cannot leak into client B's deck and that utilisation reporting stops being a spreadsheet. Entry via industry pages and case studies. Converts on: "see it on your data" demo.
**P4 — The builder** (analyst, technical marketer, ops engineer inside the customer). Not the buyer; the champion. Wants to see what building actually looks like. Needs: docs, a sandbox or video, honest limits. Converts on: sharing the site internally; docs and demo video are for them.
**P5 — The partner** (boutique consultancy/MSP principal). Wants margin and a differentiated offer. Entry via the partners page and the comparison content. Converts on: partner enquiry.
**P6 (secondary) — Public-sector digital lead.** Councils via the LGA world; needs framework routes and accreditations stated plainly. Served by the councils industry page and trust centre; converts on scoping call, expects a long cycle.
 
## 3. User journeys
 
**J1 — AI-assistant entry (now the most common first touch).** P1 asks ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini "alternatives to Copilot Studio with better data governance" or "how do I stop shadow AI". The engine cites our comparison page or shadow-AI explainer because the answer is in the first paragraph and the page is structured for extraction. Visitor lands mid-funnel, already framed → comparison page → pricing → scoping call. Requirement: every comparison/explainer page answers its question in the first 80 words.
 
**J2 — Search entry, problem-first.** P3 searches "AI tools for [industry] client confidentiality" or "[industry] AI governance". Lands on industry page → case study → packages → books the demo-on-your-data. Requirement: industry pages carry real sector vocabulary and a sector-specific proof point, not find-and-replace copy.
 
**J3 — Referral/reference entry.** Hears "as used by [70k-person enterprise]" or meets us at an IPA/RIBA event, or receives the Top Trumps deck. Goes straight to the homepage, then team page (who are these people?), then pricing. Requirement: the team page must carry the credibility load — real bios, real delivery history.
 
**J4 — The champion's loop.** P4 finds the docs or the "what you can build" gallery, plays with the sandbox video, sends the security page to their CISO and the pricing page to their boss. Requirement: every page a champion would forward has a clean one-line summary at the top and prints/pastes well.
 
**J5 — Partner journey.** P5 reads a comparison page, realises clients will ask, checks the partners page for the margin structure, enquires. Requirement: partner terms in plain numbers (the Grist model), not "let's talk".
 
**J6 — The CISO detour.** From any journey, the deal pauses while P2 checks the trust centre. Requirement: nothing in the trust centre requires a form fill except the full questionnaire pack.
 
## 4. Sitemap and page-by-page specs
 
Nav: **Product** (dropdown: How it works, Security & access control, Deployment options, Connectors, Models & BYO-key) · **Pricing** · **Industries** · **What you can build** · **Partners** · **Company** (Team, Trust centre, Writing) · CTA button: **Book a scoping call**. Footer adds docs, comparisons, legal, LinkedIn/GitHub.
 
Every page: one primary CTA, answer-first opening paragraph, FAQ block with FAQPage schema where questions are real, named author where the content is opinion, "last reviewed" date shown.
 
**Home** — audience all; job: qualify and route in 5 seconds. Hero: H1 "Let your people build with AI. Without losing control of your data." Sub: one sentence naming builders, business data, and inherited access rules. Primary CTA "Book a scoping call"; secondary "See how it works" (2-min product video, real UI). Below: customer logos (or, pre-reference, "built by the team behind [anonymised credentials]"); the three-beat distrust diagram (from the Atlas, redrawn); three outcome blocks by persona (govern shadow AI / cut the AI bill / ship internal tools weekly); package strip (three cards, prices visible, link to pricing); industry strip (six tiles); one quantified testimonial; footer CTA repeat. SEO: brand + "governed AI workspace"; Organization + Product schema.
 
**How it works** — audience P1/P4. Walkthrough in five steps (connect data via MCP/APIs with OAuth → set the access model once → people build in the workspace → humans approve real actions → everything auditable, derivatives inherit rules). Real screenshots per step, 30-second clips. CTA: scoping call; secondary: docs.
 
**Security & access control** — audience P2/P1; the most important page after home. Opens with the one-paragraph answer: agents get capabilities not credentials; per-user/per-document sandboxes; provisional actions with human commit; observation logging; derivative products inherit all of it. Then the architecture diagram, threat model (prompt injection, data exfiltration, over-permissioned agents), deployment isolation, model-provider data handling (BYO-key means your contract with the model vendor, not ours). FAQ: the ten questions off a CAIQ. CTA: download the security pack (email-gated, the only gate on the site). Schema: FAQPage, TechArticle.
 
**Deployment options** — cloud (managed by us) / your cloud (BYOC, the Snowplow pattern) / on-prem-sovereign. Comparison table, what we operate vs what you operate, data residency. CTA: scoping call.
 
**Connectors** — grid of data sources and systems (M365, Google, Slack, SQL, Dynamics, Salesforce…), each linking to a short page ("Use your [X] data, governed") that doubles as a programmatic SEO surface; "we build missing connectors as fixed-scope work" — CTA into packages.
 
**Models & BYO-key** — which models, bring-your-own-key vs managed tokens with the small markup stated, cost-per-user worked example (embed the interactive bill calculator from the Atlas — it is a genuine lead magnet and the honest version of "half the price of Copilot"). CTA: run your numbers → scoping call.
 
**Pricing** — the packages page; audience P1/P3, forwarded to finance. Structure: seat pricing first (Builder seat £40/user/mo, Consumer seat £6/user/mo, volume and public-sector discounts noted; tokens: BYO-key free or managed at cost+margin) then the three packages as cards:
- **Pilot** — fixed price, 4–6 weeks, your data, up to N builders, success criteria agreed up front; price shown.
- **Secure Landing** — the flagship: platform live in your tenancy, access model implemented, three connectors, first blueprints, training; "from £75k", 6–8 weeks.
- **Enterprise / Sovereign** — on-prem or sovereign hosting, accreditation support, custom connectors; "talk to us".
Each card: what's included, what you leave with, one CTA. Below: annual-bill comparison vs blanket Copilot (calculator), pricing-principles paragraph (why builders and consumers are priced differently — this page is quotable and engines lift it), FAQ (contracts, BYO-key, education/charity rates, what happens at renewal). Schema: Product + Offer. This is the page that must never claim "5x productivity"; it claims measurability: "every pilot ships with usage and outcome reporting — you'll know."
**Industries hub + 6–8 industry pages** — template (target ~900 words each, genuinely sector-specific): H1 "Governed AI for [industry]"; first paragraph states the sector's specific risk (multi-client confidentiality for agencies; project data and bid libraries for AEC; TPN and client IP for studios; candidate data for recruiters; DPIAs and vulnerable-person data for councils); three concrete applications with screenshots; the sector's compliance line (what we hold: CE+, ISO 27001, 42001 when true); one case study or design-partner quote; sector FAQ; CTA "See it on your [industry] data". Launch set: marketing & advertising agencies, architecture & engineering, games/VFX studios, consultancies & recruiters, councils & public sector, media & publishing.
 
**What you can build (applications hub) + programmatic pages** — the second SEO engine and the champion's page. Gallery of 20–40 short application pages from one template: "Build a [thing] on your [data]" (client-scoped pitch assistant, utilisation dashboard, bid library search, FOI-response drafter, spec checker, timesheet chaser…). Each: what it is, 60-second build video or screenshots, which connectors, which guardrails apply, "time to build: an afternoon". Cross-linked to industries. These pages answer the long-tail "AI tool for X" queries and give the engines concrete nouns to cite.
 
**Comparisons** — "Excelcion vs Copilot Studio", "vs Power Platform", "vs Retool", "vs building it yourself on Cloudflare OS", "vs blanket Copilot seats". Honest tables (say what the other tool is better at), answer-first verdict paragraph, kept current quarterly — these are the pages AI assistants cite in J1, and the freshness data says stale ones lose citations. Footer-linked, not in main nav.
 
**Partners** — the channel page. The offer in numbers: keep 100% of implementation fees, X% off licence list, Y% first-year commission; what we provide (training, sandbox, co-selling); who we want (boutique consultancies and MSPs in the target sectors). CTA: partner enquiry form.
 
**Team** — carries J3's credibility check and E-E-A-T for the whole domain. Five founders: real photographs (one consistent style; resist the temptation to use the horse deck here, but a small horseshoe Easter egg is allowed), bios written as delivery history ("built X for Y-thousand users at Z") not adjectives, LinkedIn/GitHub links, Person schema, each founder listed as author of their essays. One paragraph on the company: independent, founder-owned, senior-only, built to last — the calm-company stance is a selling point to buyers burned by vendor churn, so say it. Plus: how we work (senior people do the work; associates, not juniors), and the accreditation list with dates.
 
**Trust centre** — self-service: current certificates (CE/CE+, ISO 27001/42001 as achieved, insurance), sub-processor list, DPA download, data-residency map, EU AI Act Article 50 statement, vulnerability disclosure policy, status page link. No forms except the full questionnaire pack.
 
**Writing** (not "Blog") — the essays that do reputation work, one per founder per quarter, on the architecture-of-distrust beat: shadow AI, capability-based security for agents, why blanket Copilot rollouts fail (using the public trial data, carefully), BYOC deployment patterns, EU AI Act for the mid-market. Named authors, dates, cited sources — these earn the backlinks that drive the 0.65 domain-authority/citation correlation.
 
**Contact / Book a scoping call** — the single conversion page every primary CTA hits. Calendly-style embed plus a 4-field form (name, work email, org size, what's driving this). No phone-number requirement. Confirmation page sets expectations (30 minutes, an engineer not a salesperson) — that line measurably improves show rates and is true.
 
## 5. CTA strategy
 
One primary action per funnel stage, consistent everywhere: awareness pages (writing, comparisons, applications) → "See how it works" (video) and newsletter/RSS as the soft ask; consideration pages (product, industries, pricing) → "Book a scoping call" / "See it on your data"; evaluation support (security, trust) → "Download the security pack" (the only email gate on the site); partners → "Become a partner". Buttons say what happens next, never "Learn more" or "Get started". Sticky header CTA site-wide; end-of-page CTA restates the page's one job in the page's own vocabulary (industry pages: "Book a scoping call about your [industry] data"). No pop-ups, no chatbots pretending to be people, no exit-intent modals — the buyers are the people most allergic to them.
 
## 6. SEO / GEO plan
 
**Keyword clusters** (head terms illustrative; validate volumes at build time): governance cluster ("AI governance platform", "shadow AI", "AI access control", "AI audit trail"); build cluster ("internal AI tools", "build AI apps on business data", "Copilot Studio alternative", "Retool alternative UK"); cost cluster ("Microsoft Copilot cost per user", "Copilot alternatives UK", "AI cost management enterprise"); industry cluster ("AI for advertising agencies", "AI for architects UK", per page); sovereignty cluster ("self-hosted AI platform", "on-premise LLM platform UK", "BYOC AI deployment").
 
**GEO discipline** (applies to every page): the direct answer in the first 80 words; H2s phrased as the questions buyers actually ask; tables and definition lists for anything comparative (extraction-friendly formatting = 3x citation likelihood); one clearly-marked statistics block per major page with sourced, dated numbers (engines love citable figures — ours come from the project's graded research); every page shows a visible "last reviewed" date and the comparisons/pricing/industry pages get a real quarterly refresh (83% of commercial citations are <12 months old). Publish llms.txt alongside sitemap.xml; keep robots open to AI crawlers deliberately and note the decision.
 
**Technical:** static site, sub-second LCP, schema throughout (Organization, Product+Offer, FAQPage, TechArticle, Person, BreadcrumbList), clean URL hierarchy (`/industries/agencies`, `/build/pitch-assistant`, `/compare/copilot-studio`), hub-and-spoke internal linking (industries ↔ applications ↔ pricing), OG images per template. UK English throughout; hreflang only if/when an EU push happens.
 
**Authority:** the essays plus the founders' existing profiles; guest analysis for trade bodies (IPA, ACE, LGA-adjacent publications); the open-source contributions and Cloudflare OS partner listing linked from GitHub; the Top Trumps deck is genuinely link-bait when it ships — put it on the site.
 
**Measurement:** scoping calls booked (primary), security-pack downloads, calculator completions, AI-referred sessions tracked separately (they convert at ~4x — tag them), citation checks monthly (ask the four big assistants the J1 questions and log whether we're cited), plus the standard funnel metrics per page template.
 
## 7. Build order and content ops
 
Phase 1 (launch, ~15 pages): home, how it works, security, pricing, deployment, team, trust centre, contact, two industries (agencies, AEC), three applications, one comparison (Copilot Studio), one essay. Phase 2: remaining industries and comparisons, application gallery to 20, calculator embed, partners. Phase 3: programmatic connector pages, case studies as they exist, essay cadence.
 
Ownership: every page has a named owner among the five; the quarterly refresh is a calendar entry, not an intention; copy rule of thumb — if a sentence could appear on any AI vendor's site, cut it. All claims must trace to the project's graded research or a customer's signed-off number; the site inherits the A/B/C discipline even though grades aren't printed.
